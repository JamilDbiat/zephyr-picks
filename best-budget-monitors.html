The specs that matter (and the ones that don't)

Monitor listings are full of numbers designed to sound impressive. Most of them won't change your day-to-day experience. Focus on these:

  • Panel type — for a budget desk monitor, an IPS panel is almost always the right call. It gives accurate colour and wide viewing angles. Avoid cheaper TN panels unless you only care about raw speed for competitive gaming.
  • Resolution — 1080p is fine on a 24-inch screen. Step up to 1440p at 27 inches; text and detail look noticeably crisper, and prices have dropped into budget range.
  • Refresh rate — 60Hz is fine for work. If you game, 100Hz or 144Hz makes motion feel dramatically smoother, and you can now get it cheaply.
  • Ergonomics — the most underrated feature. A stand that adjusts height (and ideally tilt) saves your neck. If the included stand is fixed, check that it supports VESA mounting so you can add an arm later.

What you can mostly ignore at this price: extreme "1ms" response-time claims, quoted contrast ratios, and HDR badges — budget HDR rarely looks better than standard mode.

Don't forget the cable

A surprising number of budget monitors ship with only one video cable, and sometimes not the one you need. Check whether your computer outputs over HDMI or DisplayPort and make sure you have the right cable on hand — DisplayPort is usually the safer bet for hitting higher refresh rates.

The bottom line

For most desks, the 27-inch 1440p all-rounder is the best balance of size, sharpness, and smoothness under $200. Drop to the 24-inch 1080p pick to save money or add a second screen, and choose the high-refresh option if gaming is your priority.
